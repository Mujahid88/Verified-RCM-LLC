Our complete sleep medicine guide

CPAP DME billing and compliance: the 90-day rule explained.

A CPAP claim doesn't fail on the device code — E0601 is straightforward. It fails on the three things behind it: whether the qualifying sleep study actually clears the AHI/RDI bar, whether the compliance download proves adherence inside a fixed 90-day window instead of just "eventually," and whether the KX modifier on a month-four claim is backed by a re-evaluation note that was genuinely written between day 31 and day 91. This guide walks the CPAP timeline from diagnostic sleep study through the 13-month capped rental, with the exact thresholds a DME MAC audit checks first.

Reviewed by Phares A., MD · Physiotherapist, CPC (AAPC) · Part of our sleep medicine billing and coding guide

Key takeaways

  • Compliance is an all-or-nothing test inside a fixed window. Medicare requires ≥4 hours of use on ≥70% of nights during one consecutive 30-day period, achieved somewhere within the first 90 days of therapy — not an average across the whole trial, and not extendable for a patient who comes close (LCD L33718, CGS/Noridian DME MACs).
  • The KX modifier means something different in month 4 than in month 1. Months one through three attest the initial-coverage criteria are met; month four and beyond attest the initial criteria and the day 31–91 re-evaluation and adherence documentation are met (Policy Article A52467).
  • Failing the 90-day trial isn't a documentation fix. Requalifying requires an in-person re-evaluation that identifies why adherence failed, plus a repeat facility-based (Type I) sleep study — a new authorization on the same test does not restart the trial.
  • E0601 is a 13-month capped rental, a separate clock from the 90-day compliance trial — ownership transfers to the patient automatically after the 13th paid rental month, and no separate purchase claim is billed.

The qualifying sleep study: the AHI/RDI threshold that gets CPAP covered

Medicare doesn't cover a CPAP device because a patient reports snoring or daytime fatigue. It covers it because a specific test result crosses a specific line. Under LCD L33718, effective for CGS and Noridian's DME MAC jurisdictions since January 1, 2024, initial coverage of E0601 requires a covered polysomnogram or home sleep apnea test showing one of two thresholds:

Qualifying AHI/RDI thresholds for initial CPAP coverage (LCD L33718, CGS/Noridian DME MACs).
Clinical pictureAHI/RDI thresholdMinimum event count
No qualifying comorbidity documented≥15 events/hour≥30 events on the study
Documented excessive daytime sleepiness, impaired cognition, mood disorder, insomnia, hypertension, ischemic heart disease, or history of stroke5–14 events/hour≥10 events on the study

The event-count minimum matters as much as the rate. A short or fragmented study that hits 5–14 events/hour on fewer than 10 total events, or 15+ events/hour on fewer than 30 total events, does not meet the LCD's definition even though the rate looks qualifying on paper — a reviewer checking the raw study, not just the summary AHI, is exactly what a documentation audit does. When the diagnostic study itself is the question — polysomnography versus home sleep apnea testing, the G-codes involved, technologist attendance requirements — that's covered in full in our sleep study and HSAT coding guide; this page picks up from the point a qualifying study is already in hand.

Core PAP device HCPCS codes and what each requires (LCD L33718; Policy Article A52467).
HCPCSDeviceCoverage condition
E0601Single-level CPAP (including auto-titrating models billed under this code)Qualifying AHI/RDI threshold above, met by covered PSG or HSAT
E0470Bi-level PAP, without backup rateOnly after a documented E0601 trial failure at the criteria described in the LCD's initial-coverage section
E0471Bi-level PAP, with backup rateNot covered for a primary obstructive sleep apnea diagnosis under this LCD; applies to a different respiratory-failure indication

The E0470 escalation path trips up authorization staff who treat it as a comfort upgrade. It isn't billable as a first-line device for OSA — the chart has to show the patient was tried on E0601, that trial failed on its own documented terms (interface fit ruled out, pressure adjustments attempted and still inadequate), and only then does bi-level become the covered next step.

The 90-day compliance window: what "adherence" actually means

This is the single most litigated fact in CPAP billing, and it's precise enough that there's no reason to get it wrong. Adherence is defined as use of the device ≥4 hours per night on ≥70% of nights during one consecutive 30-day period, and that 30-day window has to fall somewhere within the first three months (90 days) of initial usage.

  • 1It's a 30-day window inside a 90-day trial, not a 90-day average. A patient can have two weak months and one strong month; if any single consecutive 30-day stretch inside the 90 days shows ≥4 hrs on ≥70% of nights, the adherence test is met. A patient can also fail with a decent overall average if no single 30-day window clears both bars at once.
  • 2"Close" doesn't count. A patient at 65% of nights, or averaging 3.6 hours on the nights used, has not met the criterion. There is no partial-credit provision in the LCD for a beneficiary who nearly gets there in the 90-day timeframe.
  • 3The re-evaluation has its own separate window: day 31 through day 91. The treating practitioner must conduct an in-person clinical re-evaluation no sooner than the 31st day and no later than the 91st day after therapy initiation, documenting improvement in OSA symptoms and reviewing the objective adherence data. A re-evaluation completed before day 31 doesn't count — there isn't enough download data yet to assess it honestly, and the LCD treats it as premature.
What happens at the day-91 mark, by scenario (LCD L33718; Policy Article A52467).
ScenarioResult
Adherence met in a qualifying 30-day window, and re-evaluation completed day 31–91 documenting benefitContinued coverage supported; KX modifier applies to month 4 and forward once both initial and continued criteria are documented
Re-evaluation completed after day 91, but it documents benefit and adherenceContinued coverage can begin from the date of that later evaluation — it does not retroactively cover the gap, but it isn't a dead end either
Adherence criteria not met anywhere in the 90-day windowRental coverage beyond month 3 is not supported on this trial; requalifying requires a new in-person evaluation of the failure cause plus a repeat facility-based (Type I) sleep study

⚠️ The exact administrative mechanics some practices ask about — whether a supplier can hold a claim rather than submit it without KX while waiting on a delayed compliance download — are addressed in DME MAC supplier guidance rather than the LCD itself; confirm current claim-hold procedure with the specific DME MAC (CGS or Noridian, by jurisdiction) before relying on it operationally.

The KX modifier: what it attests, and what replaces it when criteria aren't met

The KX modifier on a PAP claim isn't a formality — it's a certification that specific documentation exists in the chart, and its meaning changes at the four-month mark.

KX modifier requirements by claim month (Policy Article A52467, CGS/Noridian DME MACs).
Claim periodKX modifier attests
Months 1–3 (initial coverage)All initial-coverage criteria are met: qualifying diagnostic test, AHI/RDI threshold, valid order
Month 4 and every month after (continued coverage)Both the initial-coverage criteria and the continued-coverage criteria are met: the day 31–91 in-person re-evaluation documenting benefit, plus the adherence data from the qualifying 30-day window

When the criteria for a given month aren't met, KX isn't simply omitted and left blank — the claim needs one of two other modifiers instead: GA when a valid Advance Beneficiary Notice was obtained in advance, or GZ when it wasn't. A claim submitted without KX, GA, or GZ on a PAP line is returned as missing information; it doesn't get adjudicated at all. And appending KX when the compliance or re-evaluation documentation isn't actually in the chart is the exact fact pattern DME MAC audits target — because the KX modifier is a per-claim attestation, a documentation gap discovered on one reviewed claim routinely triggers a look-back across the entire monthly billing series for that beneficiary, not just the claim that was pulled.

Pro tip

Pull the compliance download and calendar the day-31 and day-91 dates the same week the device is dispensed, not the week the month-four claim is due. A re-evaluation scheduled reactively, once billing flags that month 4 needs KX, is the most common reason practices end up with a visit that falls one day outside the 31–91 window on either end — entirely avoidable with a scheduling reminder set at dispense.

The worked timeline: sleep study to purchase or discontinuation

Put together, the CPAP episode runs on two clocks at once — a short one that decides whether the rental continues past month three, and a much longer one that decides when the patient owns the device outright.

CPAP billing timeline from diagnosis to capped-rental conversion.

Day 0Qualifying PSG or HSAT confirms AHI/RDI at the covered threshold; treating practitioner orders E0601Order and qualifying test become the initial-coverage documentation supporting KX for months 1–3
Day 0–90Device dispensed; usage data accumulates via the device's compliance downloadRental billed monthly; KX on months 1–3 attests initial criteria only
Day 31–91In-person re-evaluation: practitioner documents symptom improvement and reviews adherence data for a qualifying 30-day windowThis visit is the continued-coverage documentation; without it, month 4 cannot carry KX
Day 91 outcomeAdherence met and re-evaluation on file, or adherence not metDetermines whether month 4 bills with KX (continued coverage) or the trial ends and GA/GZ applies going forward
Month 4 onward, if compliantRental continues on a standard capped-rental scheduleKX now attests both initial and continued criteria each month
Month 13 (paid)Capped-rental period completesOwnership transfers to the beneficiary automatically; no separate purchase claim is billed, and rental billing stops
If adherence fails within the 90-day windowRental coverage beyond month 3 is not supported on that trialRequalifying requires an in-person evaluation of the failure cause plus a repeat facility-based (Type I) sleep study — not a resubmission of the same data

⚠️ The 13-month capped-rental mechanism (payment for up to 13 continuous months with automatic ownership transfer, no separate purchase claim) reflects Medicare's general DME capped-rental payment rule rather than a provision specific to the PAP LCD; this build confirmed it through CMS-adjacent secondary sources rather than by opening the primary capped-rental regulation directly. Confirm the current capped-rental mechanics for PAP equipment against your DME MAC's supplier manual before relying on the month-13 conversion date in a patient-facing timeline.

Supply codes and the replacement schedule payers actually enforce

The device is the smallest recurring line on a CPAP patient's chart over time; the interface and consumable supplies are what generate a claim almost every month, and they're also where a frequency-limit denial is most likely because a replacement was ordered ahead of schedule.

The base E0601 unit itself carries a 5-year reasonable useful lifetime; a replacement device inside that window needs the treating practitioner's documentation of continued medical benefit, not just a supplier's note that the unit is old or the patient wants a newer model. And under the LCD's refill requirements, a supplier can't ship replacement supplies on autopilot: contact confirming continued need has to happen no sooner than 30 calendar days before the current supply is expected to run out, with the beneficiary's affirmative response documented, and delivery no sooner than 10 days before the existing supply is exhausted. A refill claim without that contact-and-affirmation trail is billed ahead of medical necessity, regardless of whether the frequency limit itself was otherwise respected.

Builds a clean audit trail
  • Pull and file the compliance download the moment a qualifying 30-day window appears, dated and labeled, rather than relying on being able to regenerate it from the device months later if audited.
  • Calendar the day-31 and day-91 boundaries at the point of dispense, and schedule the re-evaluation visit inside that window rather than around the practice's next available slot.
Invites recoupment
  • Appending KX to a month-4+ claim because the patient "seems to be doing fine," without a re-evaluation note dated inside day 31–91 and adherence data on file to match it.
  • Treating a patient who nearly met adherence as compliant, or resubmitting the same failed trial's data as if a new 90-day window had started.

Frequently asked questions

What AHI or RDI does Medicare require before it will cover a CPAP device?

A facility-based polysomnogram or a qualifying home sleep apnea test must show an AHI or RDI of 15 or more events per hour, based on a minimum of 30 events, or an AHI/RDI of 5 to 14 events per hour, based on a minimum of 10 events, with documented excessive daytime sleepiness, impaired cognition, mood disorders, insomnia, hypertension, ischemic heart disease, or a history of stroke (LCD L33718, CGS/Noridian DME MACs, effective 01/01/2024). Below those thresholds, E0601 does not meet Medicare's medical-necessity bar regardless of symptoms.

What happens if a patient doesn't meet the CPAP compliance requirement within the first 90 days?

Coverage of the rental ends. The requirement is an all-or-nothing test inside a fixed window — use of at least 4 hours per night on at least 70% of nights during any single consecutive 30-day period, achieved somewhere within the first three months of therapy — and a patient who comes close without fully meeting it in that window does not qualify for continued coverage on that trial. Requalifying is not automatic: it requires an in-person clinical re-evaluation that documents why the patient failed to adhere, followed by a repeat facility-based (Type I) sleep study, before a new trial can start (LCD L33718; Policy Article A52467, CGS/Noridian DME MACs).

What does the KX modifier attest on a CPAP claim, and what happens if it's missing?

The KX modifier attests that the documentation on file supports the coverage criteria for that month's claim — for months one through three, that the initial coverage criteria (qualifying test, AHI/RDI threshold, order) are met; for month four and every month after, that both the initial criteria and the continued-coverage criteria (the day 31–91 re-evaluation showing benefit and adherence) are met (Policy Article A52467). A claim missing KX, GA, or GZ is returned as missing information, and a claim carrying KX without the compliance and re-evaluation documentation actually on file is exactly the audit finding that turns into a post-payment recoupment on every claim in the series, not just the one reviewed.

Is a Medicare CPAP device a rental or a purchase, and when does the patient own it?

E0601 is billed as a capped rental. Medicare pays a monthly rental fee for up to 13 continuous months of medically necessary use, and ownership transfers to the beneficiary automatically after the 13th paid rental month — the supplier cannot bill a 14th rental month or a separate purchase fee for the same device. This 13-month capped-rental clock is a separate, longer timeline from the 90-day compliance trial: the compliance trial decides whether rental billing continues past month three at all, and the capped-rental rule decides when the paid-for device becomes the patient's property.
