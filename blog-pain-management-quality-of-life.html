Pain management: improving quality of life through comprehensive care

Pain is one of the most common reasons people seek medical care. While acute pain often resolves as the body heals, chronic pain can persist for months or years, impacting physical health, emotional well-being and overall quality of life. Pain Management is dedicated to diagnosing, treating and managing pain through comprehensive, patient-centered care.

Conditions commonly treated

  • ✓Chronic back and neck pain, sciatica and nerve pain
  • ✓Arthritis and degenerative disc disease
  • ✓Fibromyalgia and neuropathic pain
  • ✓Migraine and chronic headaches
  • ✓Post-surgical and cancer-related pain

Treatment approaches

Pain Management combines medical, rehabilitative and interventional treatments tailored to each patient:

  • ✓Comprehensive pain assessment and medication management
  • ✓Physical therapy and therapeutic exercise
  • ✓Epidural steroid injections and nerve blocks
  • ✓Radiofrequency ablation and spinal cord stimulation
  • ✓Behavioral health support and lifestyle counseling

Why a stepped approach usually comes first

Most pain management plans start conservative and escalate only if a less invasive option does not provide adequate relief. Physical therapy, targeted exercise and, where appropriate, anti-inflammatory medication are typically tried before an injection, and an injection is typically tried before a more invasive procedure like radiofrequency ablation or spinal cord stimulation. This is not simply caution for its own sake: the stepped approach matches the risk and cost of the treatment to how much benefit it has actually been shown to provide for a given condition, and it gives your care team information from each step that shapes the next one. If a steroid injection provides significant but temporary relief, for example, that response pattern helps confirm the diagnosis and supports moving toward a longer-lasting intervention targeting the same source.

What to track between visits

Chronic pain fluctuates, and a single description of "it hurts" at a follow-up visit gives your provider far less to work with than a tracked pattern. Note when pain is worse or better relative to activity, time of day, weather, sleep quality or stress, and rate its intensity consistently, a simple 0 to 10 scale is enough. Also track how pain affects specific activities: can you climb stairs, sit through a meal, sleep through the night. This kind of functional detail often matters more to treatment decisions than the intensity number alone, because it shows whether a treatment is actually restoring the parts of daily life that matter to you, not just lowering a number on a scale.

Final thoughts

By focusing on both symptom relief and long-term wellness, Pain Management continues to play a vital role in improving health outcomes, restoring independence and enhancing quality of life for patients of all ages.
