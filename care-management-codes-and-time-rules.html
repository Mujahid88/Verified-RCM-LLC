Our complete care management guide

Care management codes, minutes and rates: the table a biller keeps open at month end.

Every care management claim comes down to the same three questions: which code family, how many qualifying minutes or days, and whose time they were. Get those right and the claim is usually a formality. This page has the codes, the thresholds, the unit limits and the approximate 2026 Medicare payment for each.

Key takeaways

  • Only some codes take clinical staff time. For CCM that is 99490 and 99439, for PCM 99426 and 99427, and for RTM management 98979, 98980 and 98981. The provider-only codes need the practitioner’s own minutes.
  • Time is the total for the calendar month, across activities, days and staff, and it is never rounded up. 19 minutes is not 20.
  • Each minute counts toward one code only, and a staff code and the provider-only code of the same service cannot be mixed in one month.
  • Maximum units cap the month. CCM stops at 60 clinical staff minutes (99490 plus two units of 99439). PCM goes to 90 (99426 plus two units of 99427).
  • Amounts here are national 2026 Medicare figures, computed from CMS relative value units. Your locality and each payer’s fee schedule decide what you are actually paid, and 2027 amounts will be lower if the proposed conversion factor is finalized.

The rates are the 2026 non-facility total relative value units multiplied by $33.4009; the conversion factor for clinicians in a qualifying alternative payment model is $33.5675, about half a percent higher. The maximum units are Medicare’s Medically Unlikely Edits effective October 1, 2026. Two RTM pairs never go together: 98977 and 98985 are not billed for the same 30 days, and 98979 and 98980 are not billed in the same month. Under CPT’s guidance the set-up code 98975 is billed once per episode of care; Medicare’s own unit limit is one per date of service.

Chronic pain management (G3002 and G3003) is a different service from CCM and PCM: a monthly bundle of pain management whose first 30 minutes are personally furnished by the practitioner, with an in-person initial visit for the first bill, billed once a month per practitioner. CMS did not adopt clinical staff or incident-to billing for it, so vendor and clinical staff time cannot count. G3003 adds 15-minute blocks after G3002, with a unit limit of 4 per date of service. It can be billed in the same month as CCM or RTM, with practitioner time for G3002 and staff time for the others. Do not bill it together with PCM by the same practitioner: CMS has not addressed that combination and agreed PCM should not be furnished alongside other care management services by the same practitioner.

How the clock works

  • Calendar month. CCM and PCM time is the total in the calendar month. RTM device codes run on a 30-day period; RTM management time is cumulative in the month.
  • Across activities and days. Fifteen minutes of plan revision, five minutes of messaging and ten minutes of coordination on different days make thirty minutes, which supports 99426.
  • Across staff. Minutes from several clinical staff members, including an outside team working under the same billing practitioner, add together toward the same staff code, provided no minute is counted twice.
  • Any hour. Staff can do the work at any time under general supervision.
  • Never rounded. 19 minutes is not 20, and 29 is not 30.
  • One code per minute. A minute counted toward CCM cannot also count toward an RTM management code.
  • Eligible minutes only. CMS says all time counted toward CCM codes must be spent on activities that are part of the service. A 60-minute call can support 35 counted minutes.

The practitioner’s own minutes can count toward 99490 when 99491 is not billed. Complex CCM (99487 and 99489) needs the billing practitioner’s own moderate or high complexity decision-making, and 99491 and 99437 cannot be delegated. What cannot be mixed in one month is a staff code and the provider-only code of the same service: 99490 with 99491, or 99426 with 99424.

The 60-minute and 90-minute ceilings

A month with more than 60 qualifying staff minutes of CCM still bills 99490 plus two units of 99439, because the unit limit is two. PCM goes further: 90 or more qualifying minutes bills 99426 plus two units of 99427, which is $67.80 plus $108.22, or $176.02 for care management and $249.17 with set-up and device codes. Complex CCM (99487) needs the practitioner’s own moderate or high complexity decision-making and does not fit a staff-led model.

Pro tip

Time before the consent and the initiating visit never counts, so the draft care plan you build in advance is unbilled work. In month one, count only the minutes after enrollment: a 40-minute plan build plus a 35-minute counted onboarding call plus 15 minutes of follow-up messages is 90 minutes only if all of it happened after consent.

Place of service, modifiers and date of service

  • Place of service: 11 for a private office. Use 19 or 22 if the practice is a hospital-owned outpatient department, which is paid at the lower facility rate. Where an outside team’s staff sit does not change the place of service, provided they are in the United States.
  • Not telehealth: do not use place of service 02 or 10, and do not append modifier 95 or GT. These are not telehealth services.
  • Date of service: our convention is the last day of the service month for CCM and PCM, and the last day of the 30-day period for RTM device codes. CMS also allows the date the time threshold is met. Use one convention throughout.
  • Exercise-based RTM: when RTM monitors a prescribed therapeutic exercise program, bill under a therapy plan of care with the GP modifier. See RTM billing.

Payer exceptions to the table

  • UnitedHealthcare commercial: 98979 is not on the eligible list. Bill 98980 only when 20 minutes of RTM management with a live call are reached; otherwise bill no RTM management code that month.
  • Cigna: excludes RTM for every condition.
  • Plans that do not pay CCM separately: use PCM after a benefits check.
  • Plans with prior authorization requirements for specific codes: without approval, bill only the codes that are not restricted.

Frequently asked questions

Which care management codes can staff time count toward?

Clinical staff time counts toward 99490 and 99439 (CCM), 99487 and 99489 (complex CCM), 99426 and 99427 (PCM), and the RTM management codes 98979, 98980 and 98981. It does not count toward the practitioner-only codes 99491, 99437, 99424, 99425, G3002 and G3003 unless the practitioner personally does the work.

What is the maximum we can bill for CCM in a month?

For clinical staff CCM, 60 minutes: 99490 plus two units of 99439. The Medicare unit limit on 99439 is two. Practitioner-only CCM (99491 plus 99437) has its own limits.

Can we bill 99426 twice if the work took 60 minutes?

No. 99426 is billed once per calendar month. Sixty qualifying minutes support 99426 plus one unit of 99427; ninety support 99426 plus two units of 99427.

Do app messages count as time?

For CCM and PCM, clinical staff minutes spent on clinically meaningful messages count, as does any asynchronous communication that is part of the service. Automated messages do not. For RTM, message minutes that are real treatment management count toward the total but never count as the required live call.

Why are 98979 and 98985 described as new?

The CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule final rule adopted the CPT additions: 98985 for 2 to 15 days of musculoskeletal data and 98979 for the first 10 minutes of RTM management. Check that your billing system and payers have them loaded.

Are these amounts what we will be paid?

No. They are national, unadjusted Medicare amounts for 2026. Locality changes them by a few percent, Medicare pays 80% after the deductible with the patient owing 20%, and commercial payers pay their contracted rates. For 2027, CMS proposed a lower conversion factor because a one-year increase expires, so 2027 amounts will be lower if that is finalized.

What is the difference between these codes and advanced primary care management?

Advanced primary care management (G0556 to G0558) is a separate bundled code family for primary care practices that covers care management, transitional care and other services without minute counting. It is outside this guide, some payers address it by name in their policies, and a practice should not bill it and CCM or PCM for the same patient without confirming the rules for that combination.
