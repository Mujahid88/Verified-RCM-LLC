Our complete gastroenterology guide

Gastroenterology denials and appeals: the CARC-to-argument playbook.

Most denial guides stop at the reason code. That's not enough to get a claim paid — you need to know whether the pair behind it is appealable at all, what specific argument moves it, and what documentation the appeal has to carry. This guide pairs gastroenterology's five recurring CARCs with the exact appeal logic for each, plus a sample medical-necessity appeal letter structure you can adapt directly.

Reviewed by Phares A., MD · Physiotherapist, CPC (AAPC) · Part of our complete gastroenterology guide

Key takeaways

  • The NCCI modifier indicator decides whether a CO-97 or CO-18 is even worth fighting. 45378 against any therapeutic colonoscopy code carries indicator 0 — not appealable, ever. Confuse that with an indicator-1 pair and staff time goes to a claim that can never win.
  • A CO-50 appeal that doesn't name the MAC and the specific LCD or article by number rarely succeeds. "Medically necessary per CMS" is not an argument — the covered-diagnosis language in the specific policy is.
  • CO-197 (missing prior auth) is almost never appealable after the fact unless the payer's own system shows an authorization on file that simply wasn't matched to the claim — get the auth number before submission, not after the denial.
  • PT/33 mismatch and CO-4 modifier errors are corrections, not appeals. Rebill with the right modifier for the payer type rather than filing a formal appeal — it's faster and the outcome is the same.

The five denials that drive gastroenterology's write-off rate

Five CARC patterns account for most of the denial volume a GI practice sees, and each one needs a different response — some are appealable with the right documentation, some are corrections you rebill immediately, and at least one is never appealable no matter how well you write the letter. Knowing which is which before staff starts on a claim is the difference between a denials team that closes its queue and one that's perpetually behind.

Sample medical-necessity appeal letter: structure and language

A CO-50 appeal wins or loses on specificity. Here's the structure that works, built around a real example: a repeat diagnostic EGD denied for a patient with documented reflux esophagitis, billed to a payer using First Coast Service Options' coverage policy for esophagogastroduodenoscopy.

Medical-necessity appeal letter structure, section by section.
SectionWhat it contains
1. IdentifiersPatient name, member ID, date of service, claim number, denied CPT and ICD-10 codes, and the CARC/RARC combination from the remittance advice
2. Policy citationName the MAC and the specific coverage document by number — for example, "per First Coast Service Options' LCD L33583, Diagnostic and Therapeutic Esophagogastroduodenoscopy" — not "per Medicare guidelines." If the payer is commercial, cite their published medical policy number instead, using the same specificity
3. Diagnosis and clinical rationaleState the specific ICD-10 code the chart supports (e.g., K21.00, GERD with esophagitis, without bleeding — a stronger diagnosis than the unspecified K21.9) and quote the exact line from the office note or endoscopy report establishing it
4. Documentation attachedProcedure/operative report, the office visit note that established medical necessity, and any prior test results relevant to a repeat or surveillance study
5. Requested actionExplicit ask — reprocess and pay the claim as submitted — and the appeal deadline you're filing within, stated so the payer's tracking system logs it correctly
Pro tip

Before writing any CO-97 or CO-18 appeal, check whether the pair is 45378 against a therapeutic colonoscopy code, or 45381 against 45390. Both carry an NCCI modifier indicator of 0, and no documentation reverses that. If that's the pair, skip the appeal entirely and fix the scrubber rule instead — the staff time is better spent on a CO-50 or a genuinely separate-site CO-97 that can actually be won.

Appeal timelines and levels

Medicare gives 120 days from the Medicare Summary Notice or remittance advice date to file a first-level redetermination with the MAC. If that's denied, a second-level reconsideration goes to a Qualified Independent Contractor within 180 days of the redetermination decision. Commercial payer timelines vary by state and by plan — some run as short as 90 days, others up to 180 — so check the specific payer's provider manual or the explanation of benefits rather than assuming Medicare's clock applies. Missing the filing window forfeits the appeal regardless of how strong the underlying medical-necessity argument is, so track the deadline the day the denial posts, not the day staff gets to it.

Do and don't

Do
  • Check the NCCI modifier indicator for the specific pair before starting any CO-97 or CO-18 appeal.
  • Name the MAC and the specific LCD or article number in every medical-necessity appeal.
  • Attach the procedure note and the office visit note establishing the diagnosis, not just a cover letter.
  • Track the appeal filing deadline from the date the denial posts, not from when staff picks it up.
Don't
  • Don't appeal a 45378-against-therapeutic-code CO-97 or CO-18 — fix the scrubber instead.
  • Don't write a medical-necessity appeal that argues necessity in the abstract without citing a specific policy.
  • Don't treat a PT/33 mismatch or a CO-4 modifier error as a formal appeal — correct and rebill.
  • Don't wait past the filing deadline while gathering documentation — file the appeal, supplement later if the payer allows it.

Frequently asked questions

Can we ever appeal a CO-97 denial for 45378 billed with a therapeutic colonoscopy code?

No. 45378 carries an NCCI modifier indicator of 0 against every therapeutic colonoscopy code in the family, and an indicator of 0 cannot be overridden by any modifier or any amount of documentation. If that's the pair behind your CO-97, don't write the appeal — fix the scrubber rule that let the claim go out that way, and rebill without 45378 on the line. Save appeal staff time for the CO-97 denials that come from a genuinely different, non-zero-indicator pair, like 45380 against 45385 at separate lesion sites.

What has to be in a medical-necessity appeal letter to actually win?

Name the MAC and the specific LCD or article by number, not "per CMS." State the exact diagnosis code the chart supports and quote the clinical note establishing it. Attach the note itself, the procedure report, and any prior test results that support the medical necessity determination. A generic letter that argues necessity in the abstract, without naming the policy and the specific covered diagnosis, is the single most common reason a winnable medical-necessity appeal fails on the first pass.

How long do we have to appeal a Medicare gastroenterology denial?

120 days from the date on the Medicare Summary Notice or remittance advice to file a first-level redetermination request with the MAC. Commercial payer appeal windows vary by state and by plan — some run as short as 90 days, others 180 — so check the specific payer's provider manual or the explanation of benefits itself rather than assuming the Medicare timeline applies. Missing the window forfeits the appeal regardless of how strong the underlying argument was.
