Our complete care management guide

Training the people who do the work: the curriculum, what the law requires and how to check competency.

Medicare does not certify care management staff, so a practice cannot hand its team a credential and call the training done. What it can do is train to a curriculum that covers the rules the billing depends on, document completion, and check competency by listening to real calls and auditing real charts. This page sets out that curriculum and separates what is required from what is recommended.

Key takeaways

  • No federal certification exists for care management staff. CMS requires clinical staff qualified under state law, not excluded from Medicare, and located in the United States.
  • Some training is required by law or by contract: HIPAA privacy and security training for the workforce, state requirements such as Arizona medical assistant training before employment, and compliance training where a Medicare Advantage contract says so.
  • Everything else is best practice that protects the claims. Program rules, documentation and time logging are where audit findings come from.
  • Check competency, do not just record attendance. Shadowed calls, scored mock calls and chart audits show whether the training took.
  • Keep signed completion records. A practitioner-signed record of staff training is also part of the supervision evidence.

Competency checks that mean something

Completion certificates show that someone sat through a course. Competency checks show that they can do the work, and they are also the strongest evidence that the practitioner supervised training. Three methods work together:

  1. Shadowed callsA trainer or experienced nurse listens to the first calls with a short checklist: consent confirmed, goals reviewed, red flags handled, counted and uncounted segments logged correctly.
  2. Scored mock callsA scripted scenario with a planted red flag and a planted out-of-scope request, scored pass or fail on whether the staff member escalates correctly and refuses the out-of-scope request.
  3. Chart audit of the first notesThe first ten notes by each new staff member go through the scorecard in the SOP page before they are treated as routine.
Pro tip

Put a planted scenario in every mock call that tests the one thing that cannot go wrong: a patient reports new weakness or numbness, and asks the staff member whether to double up on a medication. The right answer is an immediate escalation, not a helpful reply.

Records to keep

  • Training completion by module, with the date, the trainer and a signature or an electronic acknowledgement.
  • The staff roster: credentials, licence or compact status for nurses, the state they work from, and the date of the last exclusion check.
  • US-location attestation for every staff member who works with patients.
  • Competency check results and any retraining.
  • The signed delegation protocol and each delegate’s acknowledgement where state law requires one.

An exclusion check is a search of the federal list of people barred from billing federal health care programs. Run it before hire and on a regular schedule afterward, and keep the result with the staff record.

Frequently asked questions

Does CMS require any certification for care management staff?

No. CMS requires that the staff be clinical staff qualified under state law, not excluded from Medicare, and located in the United States. Training beyond that is set by state law, payer contracts and the practice’s own compliance program.

Is HIPAA training really required for care management staff?

Yes. The HIPAA Privacy Rule requires workforce training on privacy policies, and the Security Rule requires security awareness and training. Train on hire and when policies change materially, and document it.

How often should staff retrain?

Annually for compliance and HIPAA, whenever a rule or protocol changes, and after any critical failure on a chart audit. A new Medicare final rule, such as the 2027 Physician Fee Schedule rule, is a reason to retrain the program-rules module.

Can an outside team be trained by the practice?

Yes, and the practice should keep the records. If an outside team provides the staff, the practitioner still has to be satisfied that they are qualified and trained, and Texas requires the written protocol to be given to delegates.

What does competency verification have to look like in Arizona?

For medical assistants trained through a physician-designed program, the Arizona rules call for verified competency. Shadowed calls and scored mock calls with signed results are a reasonable way to document it. Confirm the current rule text with your attorney.

Confirm before you rely on this. Training requirements depend on state law, payer contracts and your own compliance program. Most of the curriculum on this page is recommended practice, not a Medicare requirement. Verify current requirements with your healthcare attorney and compliance officer before relying on this page.
