Our complete care management guide

Enrolling patients in CCM and PCM: the initiating visit, consent and the provider note.

Enrollment is where a care management program is won or lost. The visit, the consent and the note have to exist before the first billed month, and they have to show this patient’s facts. The good news is that none of it needs a separate appointment: the follow-up visit a practice already bills can be the initiating visit.

Key takeaways

  • Enroll at the visit you already bill. A qualifying office visit (99212 to 99215), an annual wellness visit or a Welcome to Medicare visit is the initiating visit, with no second visit or second E/M.
  • The program has to be discussed at that visit. If CCM is not discussed during the visit, it cannot count as the initiating visit. We apply the same rule to PCM.
  • Consent comes before the first billed month, spoken or written, documented with the date and who obtained it.
  • Tell the patient what it costs. Cost sharing applies each month the service is billed, and for CCM only one practitioner can bill it.
  • The visit minutes never count toward the service. Visit time belongs to the visit, not to CCM or PCM.
  • Repeat the initiating visit after one year for PCM. For CCM, it is needed again when the billing practitioner has not seen the patient in the past year.

Enrolling at the regular office visit

  • The office visit is the initiating visit. CMS requires an initiating visit for new patients and for patients the billing practitioner has not seen in the past year. A comprehensive E/M office visit (99212 to 99215), an annual wellness visit, a Welcome to Medicare visit or, for CCM, the face-to-face visit for transitional care management qualifies.
  • Discuss the program at that visit. If you do not discuss CCM during the visit, it cannot count as the initiating visit. We apply the same rule to PCM.
  • One E/M only. The enrollment discussion is part of the visit you already billed. Do not bill a second E/M, and choose the visit level from that visit’s medical decision making or total time as usual.
  • Optional add-on, CCM only. If the practitioner personally performs an extensive assessment and care planning beyond the usual effort of the visit, G0506 may be billed once with the initiating visit. It is not automatic.
  • Time never double counts. Minutes spent in the office visit count toward the visit only, never toward PCM or CCM.
  • The billing month. PCM and CCM are billed per calendar month once the time threshold is met. The visit and the first PCM or CCM claim can fall in the same month, because the minutes are separate. The first claim should be for a month after consent.
  • Repeat the visit. For PCM, a new initiating visit is needed after one year to continue. For CCM, one is needed when the billing practitioner has not seen the patient in the past year.

There is also a floor: PCM cannot be billed for fewer than 30 minutes in a month, and a month without the minimum is simply not billed.

Consent

Consent can be verbal or written and has to be documented in the record before the first billed month. It is needed once, unless the patient changes practitioners. The patient has to be told:

  • What the service is and that it is available to them.
  • That cost sharing applies each month it is billed: for Original Medicare, the yearly Part B deductible and 20% coinsurance, which CMS cannot waive.
  • For CCM, that only one practitioner can bill it for them in a month.
  • That they can stop at any time, effective at the end of the calendar month.

A consent script

Dr. [name] recommends a monthly care management program for your [condition]. A care team member will check in with you by phone and in the [app name], help manage your care plan, and keep Dr. [name] updated.

This is a billed medical service: your plan’s cost sharing applies each month it is billed (Original Medicare: 20% coinsurance after the yearly Part B deductible). [CCM only: Only one practitioner can bill this program for you each month.] You can stop at any time, effective at the end of that month. Do you agree to start?

For remote therapeutic monitoring and for chronic pain management we recommend a separate consent. An RTM consent might say: “You will record your [back] pain and function in the app, and a nurse will review it and call you. This is billed separately; your plan’s cost sharing applies. Do you agree?” Do not describe any of these programs as covered by insurance, and do not offer to waive cost sharing; see compliance and audit risk.

Pro tip

Record who obtained the consent. If a medical assistant explains the program and the practitioner confirms it, the note should say both. A consent line with no name and no date is the one auditors treat as missing.

Provider enrollment note: PCM

Add this to the Assessment and Plan of the enrollment visit note. Replace every bracket with this patient’s own facts.

PCM enrollment and medical necessity.

Qualifying condition: [Condition, ICD-10 code], present since [month/year], expected to last at least 3 months.

Why the patient is at significant risk: [patient-specific facts, for example: pain interference PEG 7/10 with two falls in 6 months; emergency department visit for pain on [date]; functional decline (stopped walking outdoors); risk of acute exacerbation; candidate for surgery or procedure].

Why disease-specific care management is needed: [for example: frequent medication changes ([drug] titrated twice since [date]); complicated by [obesity, diabetes or depression]; needs ongoing coordination with physical therapy, imaging and the dietitian; requires frequent monitoring between visits].

Plan: I recommend Principal Care Management for [condition]. A disease-specific care plan is established today and will be reviewed and revised at least [monthly]. Care management will be provided by clinical staff ([credential]) under my general supervision. I will review progress at [frequency] and at office visits.

Discussion and consent: Discussed PCM with the patient today, including what the service is, that cost sharing applies each month it is billed, that only one practitioner can bill this service per month, and that the patient may stop at any time, effective at the end of the month. Patient gave [verbal/written] consent on [date]. Consent obtained by [name].

Other care management: Patient reports no other practitioner provides care management for this condition. [Not billing CCM for this patient this month.]

PCM criteria: a single high-risk condition expected to last at least 3 months that places the patient at significant risk of hospitalization, acute exacerbation or decompensation, functional decline or death.

Provider enrollment note: CCM

CCM enrollment and medical necessity.

Qualifying conditions (2 or more): 1) [Condition, ICD-10]; 2) [Condition, ICD-10]; [3) ...]. Each is expected to last at least 12 months, or until death.

Why the patient is at significant risk: [patient-specific facts for each condition, for example: knee osteoarthritis with progressive functional decline; type 2 diabetes with A1c 8.6%; obesity BMI 36 increasing joint load; risk of acute exacerbation, decompensation or functional decline].

Plan: I recommend Chronic Care Management. A comprehensive care plan covering all conditions above is established today and will be reviewed and revised at least [monthly]. Care management will be provided by clinical staff ([credential]) under my general supervision. [If applicable: I personally performed an extensive assessment and care planning today (G0506).]

Discussion and consent: Discussed CCM with the patient today, including what the service is, that cost sharing applies each month it is billed, that only one practitioner can bill CCM per month, and that the patient may stop at any time, effective at the end of the month. Patient gave [verbal/written] consent on [date]. Consent obtained by [name].

Other care management: Patient reports no other practitioner bills CCM. [Not billing PCM for this patient this month.]

CCM criteria: two or more chronic conditions expected to last at least 12 months or until death, placing the patient at significant risk of death, acute exacerbation or decompensation, or functional decline.

What to add for RTM

  • The musculoskeletal diagnosis listed first.
  • An order stating the condition, the data to be collected and how it will guide treatment.
  • The separate RTM consent.
  • A line that RTM was discussed at this visit and why it is appropriate for this patient. This costs nothing now and fits the initiating-visit requirement CMS has proposed for 2027.

Before the visit and after it

  1. Before: billing staff run the benefits check (payer rules) and send the result to the practitioner, who decides CCM or PCM with the rule in CCM vs PCM.
  2. At the visit: discuss the program, document consent, record the diagnoses with the qualifying one first, the RTM order if relevant, and the choice with its reason.
  3. After: the care team drafts goals for approval within seven days, the patient gets a copy of the care plan, and the first billed month is a later month.

Frequently asked questions

Does the patient need a separate visit before enrollment?

No. If the billing practitioner sees the patient at a qualifying visit and discusses the program there, that visit is the initiating visit. A second E/M is not billed, and visit minutes do not count toward the service.

Which visits count as the initiating visit?

For CCM, a comprehensive E/M office visit (99212 to 99215), an annual wellness visit, a Welcome to Medicare visit or the transitional care management face-to-face visit. The program has to be discussed at the visit. We apply the same standard to PCM.

Does consent have to be written?

No. Medicare accepts verbal or written consent, documented in the record before the first billed month with the date and who obtained it. We recommend a documented script so the content is consistent.

What if the patient changes practitioners?

New consent is needed when the billing practitioner changes. The new practitioner also needs their own initiating visit if they have not seen the patient in the past year.

How often do we repeat the initiating visit?

For PCM, after one year to continue the service. For CCM, when the billing practitioner has not seen the patient in the past year. If the practitioner sees the patient at least annually, the regular visits do the work.

Can we use the same note for every patient?

No. Cloned notes are an audit risk. Use the template to force patient-specific facts into each bracket, and review a sample of notes in the monthly chart audit.

Confirm before you rely on this. Medicare has no required template for these notes. The templates on this page are documentation examples, not legal advice, and the billing practitioner is responsible for the content of every note. Replace every bracket with the patient’s own facts and do not copy the same wording across patients.
